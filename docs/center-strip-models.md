# Strip landmark models

These are independent Three.js building groups from OpenStreetMap outlines and hand-built exterior details. The map still uses Google raster for the ground. Apple Maps screenshots and exterior photography informed proportions, facade colors, and placement of distinctive details; neither basemap is embedded in the models.

## Reuse a building

Open **Map layers → Inspect buildings** or **Places → Landmark Studio**, select an entry, and choose **Download .glb**. The file contains the selected meshes, textures for lettering and representative facades, and standard glTF materials. Mesh coordinates use metres with Y up and are centered on the site. The root's glTF extras record its longitude/latitude anchor and footprint attribution.

The map's live window lighting, light spills, crane animation, and satellite or road tiles are not part of the GLB. The studio uses the same tower builders with an optional standalone podium where available. Map podiums keep their opening and road clearance logic; the standalone podiums are simplified outlines. Surrounding roads, neighbors, and separate scene props are not exported as part of a hotel.

For JavaScript reuse, the shared builders are registered in `world/landmark-models.js`. Each builder returns a fresh group and leaves footprint claims off in standalone mode. Landmark Studio offers named camera views for architectural details where available. Set the Studio URL's `focus` parameter to a registered detail ID to open it directly.

## Check the map view

**Street-level Strip skyline** uses the same low perspective as a follow camera. The city layer extends its depth while preserving the basemap's x/y projection and near plane. At steep camera angles a separate ground pass blends the edge of the raster before the city draws. The skyline renders before junction detail, bridges, foliage and signal hardware are ready.

**Bellagio–Paris northbound** frames the guitar and both Flamingo marquees together. **Fashion Show & Wynn displays** and **Encore marquee & Zouk display** open the next groups of signs. The moving signs use authored sample campaigns from `world/strip-displays.js`. Each display repaints a single texture when its campaign advances. Wrapped and split panels use a continuous UV run so text stays aligned across corners and the window gap.

Use [the rebuild playbook](landmark-rebuild-playbook.md) to compare the same camera in daylight and at night, preserve mapped roads and neighboring props, and check the live map and standalone model. Carry forward the legacy renderer edits in `landmarks/vendor/` if the city is reimported. The rebuilt Cloud and Horseshoe marquee replace their old entries in `landmarks/vendor/landmarks/resorts.js`.

The Sphere mixes eight original sample campaigns into its existing rotation. The Strip's playlists use selected campaigns from the same artwork in portrait and wide formats. Choose **The Sphere** in Places and pick an ad from the dropdown to hold it on screen. Open the Sphere camera with `?sphere-ad=city-afterglow#theme=night&view=sphere` to link to a specific ad. The other IDs are `ghostbar`, `pit-lights`, `neon-dive`, `desert-miles`, `second-set`, `neon-market`, and `late-checkout`.
